## Findings

All paths are under `research/`, written `S/` below. No timing benchmarks were run; everything here comes from reading the code and re-aggregating the existing logs.

### 1. [critical] The framework-core cost is not stationary: it rises 1.6-1.9x inside every 110-frame run, and the reported medians are mid-slope values
**Location**: `S/wf2/gapfill.md` Gap 1 table; `S/wf2/explanation.md:3,90,250`; `S/v/gapfill/core/cstats.py` (median over frames 10-110); `S/v/gapfill/core/run_core.sh` (110 frames per process).
**Finding**: The verdict's load-bearing number "0.171 / 0.803 / 2.15 ms for 501 / 2,001 / 5,001 elements" is the median of a series that climbs monotonically from the first frame to the last in every run, on every executor (12 threads, 1 thread, default lane, bang-free binary) and in every stage (view, layout, paint all rise together). Nothing about the work changes between frames (nq and ns are constant). The report's own Gap 4 conclusion ("the allocator slowdown is not a killer for a UI that rebuilds only a few thousand chars per frame") was drawn from a workload of ~2,000 chars/frame, while the core at 5,001 elements rebuilds ~96k chars of `rowtext` strings plus ~80k `RCon` run nodes and ~80k `SpCon` sprite nodes per frame, i.e. exactly the few-MB-per-frame String churn the report says triggers #1007. Nobody noticed because `cstats.py` reports a median.
**Evidence**: Per-11-frame medians (µs) over all 110 frames, from `S/v/gapfill/core/mx/*.out`:
- `one-cpu12-125`: 112, 128, 144, 158, 165, 172, 189, 177, 182, 178 (+59%)
- `one-cpu12-500`: 517, 637, 762, 828, 845, 890, 904, 914, 915, 921 (+78%)
- `one-cpu12-1250`: 1327, 1766, 1916, 2114, 2259, 2326, 2400, 2446, 2463, 2486 (+87%, still rising at frame 110)
- `one-cpu1-1250`: 1344, 1720, 1900, 2093, 2203, 2331, 2367, 2424, 2464, 2497 (same shape with 1 thread, so not pool contention)
- `staged-cpu12-1250_r1`: view 445 to 637, layout 900 to 1109, paint 451 to 727 (first 20 vs last 20 measured frames).
110 frames is under one second at 120 Hz. Whether this plateaus (working-set effect) or keeps growing (#1007 shape, which the report reproduced at 2.7-18x) is unknown, and no RSS was logged (`core_main.bend` has no `Rss.kb`). The chain "core measured 0.17-2.15 ms" -> "about 3 ms total" -> "well under 8.3 ms" rests on an understated, non-steady-state number.
**Suggestion**: Rerun `core` at 500 and 1,250 rows for 2,000-5,000 frames with `Rss.kb` per frame; report per-100-frame medians and state plainly whether it plateaus and at what multiple of the first-frame cost. Report the plateau, not the median of the ramp. If it does not plateau, the verdict's "framework logic can be pure Bend" claim needs per-line text caching built into the measured core, not just mentioned as a design rule.

### 2. [warning] The headline P3 frame numbers are headless numbers at sustained GPU clocks; the only in-window measurement shows 5-10x more GPU time, and the design serializes CPU and GPU, so that time sits on the critical path
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:8,107` ("0.42-0.47 / 0.62-0.71 ms", "Estimated total with a 5k-element core: about 3 ms"); `S/wf2/gapfill.md:106` ("stays well under 8.3 ms"); `S/v/gapfill/p3/p3fx.c:283-285` (`[cb commit]; [cb waitUntilCompleted];` inside the effect).
**Finding**: `p3_go` blocks on `waitUntilCompleted` before returning the lists, so every frame is build + walk + encode + full GPU time + present, sequentially. The report then adds the core (2.15 ms) to the *headless* renderer (0.7 ms) to get "about 3 ms". But the in-window GPU time is not 0.34 ms. The report lists "2.55 ms vs 0.34 ms, unexplained" as a blocker and does not propagate it into the estimate. The measure agent already found the mechanism for bangs ("idle gaps slow the GPU lane 3-4x... consistent with clock/power ramp-down", plus 35-65% external GPU load), and it applies identically to a 0.3 ms render pass issued once per 8.3 ms.
**Evidence**: `S/v/gapfill/win/p3w_r{1,2,3}.err`, frames 5+: GPU medians 1,746 / 2,930 / 2,552 µs; p90 3,635 / 4,082 / 4,080; max 4,734 / 5,541 / 5,377. Headless at the same resolution: 342 µs (`S/v/gapfill/p3/mx/m0-*_3024x1964_n256_r*.err`). Combining the measured pieces as the serialized design actually runs them: core plateau at 1,250 rows (2.25-2.5 ms, finding 1) + in-window GPU p90 (4.1 ms) + build/walk/encode (~0.1 ms) is about 6.5 ms; at the GPU max it is about 8.0 ms. That is the whole 120 Hz budget, for 256 cards and 2,000 glyphs of 8x16 px. "Well under 8.3 ms" and "about 3 ms" are not supported by the in-window data.
**Suggestion**: Make the in-window GPU distribution (median, p90, max) the headline renderer figure; recompute the end-to-end estimate from it; and either measure the GPU time with a realistic glyph load or say the margin is unknown. Test whether `P3_OWNQ=1` or a persistent-clock workaround changes it, so the reader knows whether this is DVFS or contention.

### 3. [warning] "Affine typing keeps the Scene alive while the GPU reads it" is only true because the effect blocks; the in-place heap read is incompatible with CPU/GPU overlap and maximizes exposure to the report's own top-ranked risk
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:94`; `S/wf2/explanation.md:203-207` (risk 1, "most likely killer"; mitigation "copy fields into a packed buffer... estimated at tens of µs, not measured"); `S/v/gapfill/p3/p3fx.c:179-181,188-211,285,294`.
**Finding**: Lifetime safety in the prototype comes from `waitUntilCompleted` at line 285, not from the type system. The moment the renderer is made asynchronous (which is what finding 2 says is needed to take 2.5-5.5 ms of GPU time off the CPU path), the program sinks the lists (`p3.drop` in `main_p3.bend`) and Bend's allocator reuses those words for the next frame while the GPU may still be reading them through an `Untracked`, no-copy `MTLBuffer` over `e.mem`. The two ways out are holding two or three Scenes alive in Bend (not prototyped, and it means the core must thread old Scenes through its loop) or copying fields into a packed instance buffer during the walk, which is the standard design, decouples the shaders from `term_rfc`/`LOC_MASK`/F32-in-low-word, and is the mitigation the report itself proposes for the "most likely killer" risk. The in-place read saves only that copy (2,257 primitives x 8-20 words, on the order of 20-50 µs). Recommending the in-place variant as the architecture, rather than as a measurement trick, does not follow from the evidence.
**Evidence**: `p3fx.c:179-181` wraps the whole heap `newBufferWithBytesNoCopy:e.mem ... HazardTrackingModeUntracked`; `p3fx.c:294` returns the lists; `io_sync()` at `p3fx.c:256` only does `fflush(stdout)` (`S/b/bend/bend2/comp.ts:5442-5446`), so the implied "sync with the evaluator" is actually provided by `io_step`'s serialization, not by this call. The copy variant was never measured.
**Suggestion**: Measure the packed-copy variant (it is a 30-line change to `p3_walk`) and recommend it; present the in-place read as the thing that was measured, not as the design.

### 4. [warning] The Retina in-window result was produced on a Bend runtime patched with PR #1108, which HEAD cannot do; the window half of the "one Objective-C effect" was never prototyped on stock Bend
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:8` ("In a real Retina window it held 120 Hz"); `S/v/gapfill/p3/p3win.c:9414,9511,9537-9563` (`backingScaleFactor`, `window_make(..., bool resizable, ...)`, `NSWindowStyleMaskResizable`); `S/wf2/explanation.md:217` (gotcha: a program without `Window.frame` fails to compile on `CID_KEY`).
**Finding**: The gapfill says it used the PR #1108 window code; the verdict's lead paragraph does not. Stock HEAD opens a fixed-size 1x window (C5 confirmed), and PR #1108 is open, labeled `later`, 74 commits behind and does not apply cleanly. The recommended design needs a custom window effect (resize, HiDPI drawable, own event drain) on stock Bend. Nothing in the prototypes opens a window from a user effect; the IME shim attaches a subview to Base's window, and the P3 present effect reuses Base's `CAMetalLayer`. The compile coupling (BendView references `CID_KEY/CID_MOUSE/CID_LOOK`) is a known trap on exactly that path.
**Evidence**: `p3win.c` contains 16 hits for `backingScaleFactor|open_resizable|Resize`; stock `S/b/bend/bend2/effs/window.c` has none (C5 skeptic). The build in `S/v/gapfill/build.sh` uses `S/d/bend` which is at 565d7fd, so the PR hunks were applied to the emitted C or a copy; either way the binary that produced "120 Hz at 2x" is not a HEAD binary.
**Suggestion**: State the dependency in the verdict and either prototype `Win.open_2x` as a user effect on stock HEAD (NSWindow + CAMetalLayer with `contentsScale`, plus the `Window.frame` reachability workaround) or move "resizable HiDPI window" from "measured" to "unprototyped".

### 5. [warning] The core and the renderer were never connected: their Scene formats are incompatible, the glyph representation P3 was timed with is explicitly not the production design, and the "about 3 ms" sum stitches 2,000 glyphs onto a core emitting 80,000
**Location**: `S/v/gapfill/core/core_lib.bend:425-427` (`SpCon{x: F32, y: F32, gid: U32, c: U32}`); `S/v/gapfill/p3/ui_common.bend` `GCons{sx, sy, c, r0..r15}`; `S/v/gapfill/p3/p3fx.c:94-99` (`glyph_fs` reads 16 inline row words `m[o+3+dy]`); `S/wf2/explanation.md:95,107`.
**Finding**: P3's shaders cannot draw the core's Scene (no inline bitmaps), and the core cannot produce P3's (it has no atlas). The report says a production version "would keep the glyph atlas texture on the C side... and put tile ids in the Scene", which is a texture-sampling renderer plus a C-side atlas manager with CoreText rasterization on miss and per-frame texture uploads; none of that exists or was timed. Meanwhile the core at 5,001 elements emits 79,985 sprites per frame, 40x the 2,000 P3 was timed with (and more than fit on a 3024x1964 screen at 8x16 px). The P3 2x test also used 8x16-px glyphs (4x8 pt, unreadable); a realistic 16x32 8-bit glyph load is 4x the fragment area per glyph. The "no damage tracking needed at Retina" claim is for an unrealistically light text frame.
**Evidence**: `nq 1251 ns 79985` in every `core/mx/*1250*.out`; `ng 2000` in every `p3/mx/*.err` and `win/p3w_*.err`; `S/wf2/gapfill.md` Gap 2 used 16x32 8-bit glyphs for P2 but Gap 3 did not for P3.
**Suggestion**: Drive `P3.present` from `core_lib`'s Scene (add `gid` to the glyph path with a real atlas texture) and time that end to end in the window. Until then, label "about 3 ms" as a sum of two unintegrated prototypes.

### 6. [warning] The shape cache the design requires has a measurable per-frame cost that the report measured and then left out of every estimate
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:87` ("the shape cache is keyed per line"); `S/wf2/gapfill.md` Gap 4 ("map 0.633 -> 0.672 ms" for 200 keys); `S/v/gapfill/drift/main_drift4.bend:42-58`.
**Finding**: A per-line shape cache over cons-list `String` keys means one `Map.get` per text row per frame (plus `Map.set` on misses). drift4 measured 200 `Map<String,U32>` get+set pairs at 0.633 ms, about 1.6 µs per operation on a 200-entry map, including building the key string. The core benchmark has 1,250 text rows at 5,001 elements; a get-only pass at that rate is roughly 2 ms per frame, comparable to the entire measured core, and it grows with log(entries) and key length. No estimate includes it, and the core benchmark uses fixed per-char advances (`adv` at `core_lib.bend:144`) so it never pays for a lookup.
**Evidence**: `S/v/gapfill/drift/d4.out`: `map_us 350-576` on the first frames, 0.633 ms median per the report; `ekey` builds `"element/" ++ U32.show(k) ++ "/state"` per lookup as a content-keyed cache would.
**Suggestion**: Add a String-keyed shape-cache lookup per text node to `core_lib` (or key by a U32 hash in the trie) and re-time; put the result in the end-to-end estimate.

### 7. [warning] "About 82% Bend" is a module-attribution ratio over zui's Rust line counts with arbitrary boundary choices; it swings roughly 69-85% under equally defensible choices and is not a measure of what the Bend project would write
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:10,14,107,279`; derivation quoted in `S/wf2/all.json` (premise-estimates): `67,358 / 82,541`.
**Finding**: The numerator counts as "Bend" `gpui/src/platform.rs` (2,886 lines of platform trait contracts, which the report says become the ObjC shim's contract) and `gpui/src/platform/` (3,786: keyboard, keystroke, app_menu, test and bench platforms, scap screen capture), `text_system` (5,127, half of which P3 moves to the C-side atlas/CoreText), `executor.rs` (498, Bend's runtime replaces it), `svg_renderer.rs` (458, resvg). Moving those out gives about 57k/82.5k = 69%. The denominator excludes taffy (22,150 lines) which GPUI imports but a Bend version must write (flex, grid, block); adding it to both sides gives about 85%. Rust lines are also not Bend lines: the sketch needed continuation shims for 14 of 84 defs and has no macros for the ~114 style methods. Finally, by the same line count GPUI itself is about 97% Rust (shaders 4,530 of 157k), with its ObjC calls written in Rust via objc2; the Bend version puts the entire platform layer in a second language. So the honest statement is weaker than the verdict's lead paragraph: by module attribution over zui, somewhere around 70-85%; by code the project would actually write, unknown; and structurally less single-language than GPUI is.
**Evidence**: Line counts from `S/a/zui/crates/gpui/src`: platform.rs 2,886; platform/ 3,786; text_system/ 3,921 + text_system.rs 1,206; executor.rs 498; svg_renderer.rs 458; taffy 22,150 (C18 skeptic).
**Suggestion**: Replace "about 82%" with a range and the method; or report two numbers (module attribution vs code-to-write) and say neither is a property of the Bend project yet.

### 8. [warning] The effort estimates are uncalibrated by the report's own admission and were moved from P2 to P3 without re-derivation
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:107,243,280`; `S/wf1/claims.json` paths P2 ("4-8 engineer-months") and P3 ("P2 plus 1-2 months for the renderer").
**Finding**: In wf1, P3 cost more than P2. In wf2, P3 is the recommended path, carries P2's old "4-8 months" unchanged, and P2 becomes "P3 effort plus Bend raster and damage machinery". The flip is argued ("probably not more work than P2") but no new sizing was done. The ledger says "Neither figure has a measured framework-scale data point", and the prior-art skeptic says backplane's history calibrates only the shim/raster layer, not the framework layer. The prototypes behind "4-10 weeks for a demo because the riskiest pieces exist" total about 1.5k lines of Bend and about 1k lines of C/ObjC, and this review finds the core cost non-stationary (1), the renderer's production form unprototyped (3, 5), the HiDPI window unprototyped on HEAD (4) and the shape cache unmeasured (6). Items the usable-subset estimate must cover and that have no prototype: clipping/content masks (P3's fragment shaders have none), scrolling with clip, images/texture sampling, shadows and paths, resize, a real event drain, and a 1-3 minute rebuild loop at framework scale (`explanation.md:210`).
**Evidence**: `wc -l S/v/bend-language/sketch/ui.bend` = 739; `S/v/gapfill/p3/p3fx.c` = 349 lines (61 of MSL); `S/v/gapfill/ime-event-loop/ime.c` ~16 KB; `S/v/gapfill/ct/shape.c` ~100 lines.
**Suggestion**: Either drop the month ranges or present them as uncalibrated guesses with the explicit list of unbuilt pieces above; do not reuse P2's number for P3.

### 9. [nit] "0% over 12.5 ms" and "no missed frames" are a rounding of 1 of 345 frames; 20-25% of frames exceed 8.6 ms
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:8,107`; `S/v/gapfill/winstats.py` (`:.0f` on the mean of per-run fractions).
**Evidence**: `S/v/gapfill/win/p3w_r1.out`, frames 5+: max 13,992 µs (one frame over 12.5 ms); 28/115, 28/115, 22/115 frames over 8,600 µs across the three runs. Vsync jitter makes the latter benign, but "no missed frames" overstates 1/345.
**Suggestion**: Report "1 of 345 frames over 12.5 ms".

### 10. [nit] The P2-vs-P3 speedup range is stated three different ways, and the whole ratio is attributed to "Bend shading the pixels" although 15-20% of the P2 frame is Base's `Image.drop!` bang and the Blit present kernel
**Location**: `S/wf2/gapfill.md:106` ("12-35x"); `S/wf2/explanation.md:10` ("16-39x"); `:112` ("16-20x ... 23-39x").
**Evidence**: From the tables: 1x 7.84-8.34 / 0.42-0.47 = 16.7-19.9x; 2x 16.48-24.22 / 0.62-0.71 = 23.2-39.1x. The gapfill's "12-35x" does not match its own table. At 2x bin-gpu, drop 3.19 ms + present 0.83 ms of the 24.22 ms are Image-path costs, not shading (`S/wf2/all.json` measure results_table).
**Suggestion**: Use one range (16-39x end to end) and say what it includes.

### 11. [nit] "One Objective-C effect file" understates the native code the report itself sizes at ~15k zui-equivalent lines
**Location**: `S/wf2/explanation.md:5,10`.
**Evidence**: The prototypes already span `ime.c`, `shape.c`, `p3fx.c`, `cat/text.c`, and a replacement for Base's `window.c` is still needed (finding 4); the report's own estimate for this layer is 10,545 lines of glue plus 4,638 of renderer and MSL.
**Suggestion**: Say "an Objective-C platform layer of several thousand lines" rather than "one effect file".
